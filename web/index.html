What this map shows

Running speed of Lviv city buses, aggregated into 25-metre grid cells from GTFS-RT vehicle positions archived every ten seconds. Each dot is one cell: green means buses move quickly there, red means they crawl. Use the hour slider to compare the morning peak against the middle of the night, the month picker to compare months, and the day selector to separate weekdays from weekends — city-wide mean speed at 17:00 is about 16.6 km/h on a weekday against 21.4 km/h at the weekend, so averaging the two together hides the rush hour entirely.

Congestion against raw speed

The Statistic selector offers more than an average. The median ignores the tail of waits at lights; the 15th and 85th percentiles show a bad day and a good one; their difference measures how unpredictable a stretch of road is. "% of free-flow" divides each cell's speed by that same cell's own 85th-percentile speed across the whole archive, which separates congestion from slowness: a narrow old-town lane that runs at 20 km/h at three in the morning is not congested, while a ring road down to 22 km/h from 40 is. Clicking any cell shows every statistic for it along with its speed hour by hour.

How the figures are produced

Only buses are counted. Lviv trolleybuses are published in the schedule feed as route_type=3, the same code as buses, so they are excluded by route name; trams are excluded by route type. Positions within 40 metres of a stop served by the vehicle's own trip are dropped, which turns dwell time at stops into gaps rather than red dots, while the stop on the opposite side of the street — a different stop served by the other direction — still counts as running road. Vehicles whose own timestamp lags the feed by more than two minutes are discarded as stale, and repeated reports of the same vehicle at the same instant are counted once.

Source data: the Lviv public transport GTFS-RT feed, archived to object storage. Basemap: OpenStreetMap.
